In Plato's *[Phaedrus]*(https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/phaedrus.html),
the god Theuth invents writing and brings it before King Thamus, expecting
praise. Instead, Thamus warns that this new invention will not make people 
wiser, only more convinced of their own wisdom. Those who rely on it, 
he says, will carry the appearance of knowledge without ever doing the work 
of possessing it.

> They will appear to be omniscient and will generally know nothing; they will 
> be tiresome company, having the show of wisdom without the reality.
> — Plato, *Phaedrus*

Most importantly here, Plato is not saying that reading is useless. The
myth namely describes writing as useful as a *reminder* for someone who
already knows something. The skepticism is aimed towards treating this
written record as a substitute for _understanding_.
